Into the Woods

A personality quiz told as a short story… and every move counts.

What is your name?

You're meeting up with friends to hike at 7 a.m. What time are you waking up?

A bear steps onto the trail, thirty feet away. It's looking right at you. What do you do?

A claw grazes your arm. You grab the bear spray from your bag and run. Which way?

The bear is gone. So are your friends. Your phone is almost dead. What now?

Your phone is dead. What do you do?

Through the trees, you spot an orange tent. A campsite: a cooler, a camping chair, a backpack hanging off a branch. Nobody's around. What do you do?

The sun is setting. You find a big fallen tree that would make a decent shelter. What do you do?

It's dark now. You're starving, and your throat is dry. What do you do?

Twenty minutes later, the tree bark starts to breathe, and the stars leave little trails. What now?

Tall, dark shapes stand at the edge of the trees. Very still. Watching you.

A voice drifts out of the dark, in your friend's voice: "you... come here. We found the way out." But it says your name flat, like it's reading it off a card.

It's pitch black, and you can't find your shelter. Which way do you go?

The woods go quiet. Sunrise is hours away. How do you get through the night?

It's a key. A tiny one, no bigger than your thumbnail, with a little star on it that shines in the moonlight. What is it?

Between two roots of the fallen tree, there's a moss-green door the size of your palm, with a snail-shell knocker and a star-shaped keyhole. Warm light glows through its round window. What do you do?

A tiny fairy stands in the doorway, in an acorn cap and a maple-leaf cardigan. "I dropped that key out the window last week," he says. "I owe you one favor. Don't be weird about it." What do you ask for?

A ranger wraps a foil blanket around you and hands you hot chocolate. "So. What happened out there?"
